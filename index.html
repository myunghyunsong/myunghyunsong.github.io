Job Market Paper

Examiner and Judge Designs with Multiple Treatments and Instruments

Abstract

Designs exploiting quasi-random assignment of cases to judges and other examiners are increasingly popular. Commonly used stringency instruments generally fail to identify pairwise causal effects with multiple unordered treatments. The effects for compliers moved between any two treatments are of interest. Continuous instruments that each move cases into only one treatment identify these effects. These designs rarely provide such instruments. We build a discrete choice model in which examiners assess each treatment using the same case characteristics with examiner-specific coefficients and unobserved components identically distributed across examiners. The coefficients are identified using the Slutsky symmetry of propensities implied by examiners' assessment maximization and additive unobserved components. Examiners' predicted assessments provide instruments satisfying these conditions given case characteristics. We estimate these effects from outcome regressions linear in case characteristics, with coefficients varying with the instruments, and develop an inference procedure. Revisiting a recent study of Virginia courts, we find different patterns in how noncarceral and carceral convictions affect reoffending.
